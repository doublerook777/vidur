What is Vidur?

A khata for kirana shops that you talk to. Mid-sale, hands full, say “Ramesh ka 340 udhaar”. Vidur hears it, asks one short question if anything is unclear, and saves the entry with its time. The ledger lives only on the phone, encrypted, and the server stores nothing.

Live demo: hold the circle and speak

  1. Hold and speak

    Press and hold the circle. Say it the way you would to a helper at the counter: “Mohan ka teen sau chalis rupaye udhaar.” Let go.

  2. It asks, it never guesses

    Two Rameshes? A name it has never heard? A number it is not sure of? Vidur asks one short question, and saves nothing until you answer.

  3. Fix it, by voice or by hand

    Say “nahi, 500” or “galat”. Or tap the three dots on any entry to remove it, or add one yourself with the date and time.

  4. Your khata, your phone

    The ledger is encrypted on your phone. Transcripts are kept for 24 hours at most, and one control deletes everything.

Language

  1. While you talk

    Your voice streams to Gnani’s speech recognition as you speak. Our own parser reads the amount and whether it is credit or payment, so no language model decides money.

  2. Nothing unclear is saved

    A new name is only created after you say yes, and an unanswered question is dropped after 30 seconds. In our test of 240 noisy synthetic recordings, no amount was saved wrong without a question.

  3. Every entry has a time

    Each entry shows when the money moved, and when it was written down if that was another day. Removing an entry can be taken back for a few seconds.

  4. The server keeps nothing

    Reminders are opt-in per customer, polite, at most one a week, and Vidur only prepares the text. You share it yourself, and nothing is ever sent for you.

Spoken the way people talk

Hindi, Hinglish, English and Kannada work at full quality. Speech is code-mixed by nature: “Ramesh ka 340 credit” is a perfectly normal sentence, and Vidur reads amounts in spoken Indian forms like “dedh sau” and “dhai hazaar”. Seven more languages (Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Marathi, Bengali, Gujarati, Punjabi) are planned, not shipped.

Privacy

  • The ledger lives only on your phone, encrypted. The server stores nothing about your entries.
  • Transcripts are kept in memory only and purged after 24 hours. There is a delete-all control.
  • No phone numbers are stored. Reminders are polite, opt-in only, and capped at one per customer per 7 days.
  • Vidur does not rate customers, does not shame anyone, and does not advise on lending or interest.

How it is built

A thin phone app (Preact) talks to a speech pipeline over WebSocket: streaming speech-to-text, a small deterministic parser for amounts and direction, and a dialogue engine that holds anything unclear as pending until you answer. An LLM fallback exists for hard cases, and every amount it returns is re-derived from your transcript before anything is saved. Money is integer paise everywhere. The ledger is event-sourced and encrypted on the device.

Honest limits

  • Speech recognition is not perfect. Vidur is built to ask when unsure rather than record a wrong amount, but mistakes still happen, so check the entry.
  • Hindi, Hinglish, English and Kannada only. Other languages are on the roadmap.
  • Reminders work only for customers who opted in, and nothing is sent automatically. You tap to share.
  • The browser build cannot hide its key from code running on the same page; entries never leave the device, but treat the demo as a demo.
  • Latency depends on the network. On a bad connection, add the entry by hand.
