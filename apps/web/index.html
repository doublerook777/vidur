Say it, don’t write it.

Vidur is a voice-first khata for Indian kirana shops. Mid-sale, hands full, the shopkeeper says “Ramesh ka 340 udhaar” and Vidur hears it, asks one short question when anything is unclear, saves the entry, and shows it on screen as a receipt. Everything is undoable by voice. The ledger lives only on the phone, encrypted, and the server stores nothing.

How it works

  1. You press and hold the mic and say the entry — “Ramesh ka 340 udhaar, kal 200 diya tha”.
  2. Vidur listens, works out the amounts and the direction (udhaar vs jama), and if anything is unclear it asks one short question instead of guessing.
  3. The entry is saved on the phone and shown as a receipt you can read at a glance. Say “nahi, 430” to fix it — everything is undoable by voice for 10 seconds.

Languages

Hindi, Hinglish, English and Kannada are supported at full quality. Speech is code-mixed by nature — “Ramesh ka 340 credit” is a perfectly normal sentence, and Vidur reads amounts in spoken Indian forms like “dedh sau” and “dhai hazaar”. Seven more languages (ta, te, ml, mr, bn, gu, pa) are planned, not shipped.

Privacy

  • The ledger lives only on your phone, encrypted on the device. The server stores nothing about your entries.
  • Transcripts are kept in memory only and purged after 24 hours. There is a delete-all control.
  • No phone numbers are stored. Reminders are polite, opt-in only, and capped at one per customer per 7 days.
  • Vidur does not rate customers, does not shame anyone, and does not advise on lending or interest.

Architecture

A thin phone app (preact) talks to a speech pipeline over WebSocket: streaming speech-to-text, a small deterministic parser for amounts and direction, and a dialogue engine that holds anything unclear as pending until you answer. An LLM fallback exists for hard cases, and every amount it returns is re-derived from your transcript before anything is saved. Money is integer paise everywhere. The ledger is event-sourced and encrypted on the device.

Honest limits

  • Speech recognition is not perfect. Vidur is built to ask when unsure rather than record a wrong amount, but mistakes still happen — check the receipt.
  • Hindi, Hinglish, English and Kannada only. Other languages are on the roadmap.
  • Reminders work only for customers who opted in, and nothing is sent automatically — you tap to share.
  • The browser build cannot hide its key from code running on the same page; entries never leave the device, but treat the demo as a demo.
  • Latency depends on the network. On a bad connection, use the keypad entry.
